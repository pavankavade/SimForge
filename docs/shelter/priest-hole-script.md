# How a Staircase Becomes a Priest Hole

OmnixxLabs short (`src/shelter/priest-hole`, composition `PriestHole`). Our retelling of a viral
"How a staircase becomes a priest hole" short (X, 2026-11-22, ~1.9M views): same story beats and
cutaway-house look, our own words, a new hook, and the realistic human cast (`PaladinActor`)
instead of the reference's game-style figures.

## Hook

The reference opens on the hiding ("If you hide under your own staircase when soldiers come…").
Ours opens on the threat arriving at *your* door with a clock on it, the channel's best-performing
pattern (docs/omnixxlabs.md: home + modern-feeling fear + second person):

> Priest hunters are pounding on your front door, and you have thirty seconds to disappear.

## Facts

- Elizabethan and Jacobean England (from 1585 a Catholic priest in England could be executed, and so
  could anyone who sheltered him). Searches were made by pursuivants, the "priest hunters".
- Nicholas Owen, a Jesuit lay brother and carpenter, built many of the surviving hides; he favoured
  brick and stone so that tapping gave no hollow sound, and built double hides (a decoy hide with a
  second one behind it). He was taken at Hindlip Hall in 1606 and died under torture in the Tower
  without giving any away.
- Searchers sounded walls for hollow places and compared inside and outside measurements of houses.
- At Hindlip (1606) the hidden Jesuits Garnet and Oldcorne were fed warm drinks (caudles, broth)
  through a reed or quill passed through a little hole in a chimney from the next room; the search
  lasted eight to twelve days (sources differ), so "a week" is safe.
- Baddesley Clinton has a hide made in a former medieval sewer; Harvington Hall has seven hides,
  including ones reached through the main staircase and on the floor of a garderobe.
- The crooked step and the cat are the story's flourishes, not historical claims.
