## Facts (to verify before voicing)

- WW1 observation-post ("OP") trees: introduced by the French camouflage section in 1915 and
  adopted by the British (painter Solomon J. Solomon led early British work).
- Method: a real shattered tree near the line was photographed and sketched in detail; a hollow
  steel cylinder was built in sections, clad in real bark or painted to match, with an internal
  ladder and small viewing slits near the top.
- The swap happened at night, the real tree cut down and the copy erected in its place, with noise
  covered by gunfire; the work had to be finished before first light.
- The observer reached the base through a trench or tunnel and reported by field telephone.
- Surviving examples are held by the Imperial War Museum and the Australian War Memorial (check
  exact provenance and dimensions before quoting any numbers on screen).
