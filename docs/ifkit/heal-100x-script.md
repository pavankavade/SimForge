## Numbers behind the lines

All healing times are typical normal times divided by 100. The energy figures are estimates; there is
no good primary study of calories per injury, so the script keeps them round and says "about".

- **Paper cut:** about 2–3 days normally (72 h), so about 43 min at 100x. The line says "forty minutes".
- **Scraped knee:** 1–2 weeks (336 h at two weeks), so about 3.4 h. Its extra cost is small: a few
  hundred calories over the week, which the script calls "a candy bar".
- **Bruise:** about 2 weeks, so 3–4 h ("gone by lunch" for a morning bruise).
- **Kid forearm fracture:** 4–6 weeks in a cast for younger kids, 6 weeks for teens, with full healing
  by about 8 weeks. The script uses 6 weeks (1,008 h), so about 10 h.
- **Fracture energy:** a long-bone fracture is commonly quoted as raising resting energy use by about
  15–20%. That figure is from secondary sources; small hip-fracture studies are less clear. A 12-year-old's
  resting burn is about 1,400 kcal/day, so 15–20% is 210–280 kcal/day, and over 42 days that's about
  9,000–12,000 kcal. The script says "about ten thousand extra calories".
- **In one night:** 10,000 kcal over 10 h is about 1,000 kcal/h. An elite marathon runner burns about
  1,000–1,100 kcal/h, so "about as hard as a marathon runner works" fits.
- **Heat:** 1,000 kcal/h is about 1,160 W of extra heat. That is why the line says he burns up like a
  fever. In reality he would overheat badly; the script keeps it to sweating and a fever.
- **Savings:** body fat stores about 7,700 kcal/kg. If about 10,000 kcal comes from reserves before the
  food is digested, that's about 1.3 kg, which the line rounds to "more than a kilo of itself". Stomach
  emptying and absorption take hours, so the reserves pay first.
- **Bone knits in whatever shape it is in:** a fracture has to be lined up (reduced) before the soft
  callus forms, which normally takes about 1–2 weeks. At 100x that's about 3–5 h, so a long ER wait
  really matters. Kids' bones remodel some angulation, but the line stays true without that detail.
- **Muscle:** after big trauma the body breaks down muscle protein for repair when intake falls short.
  That's the "then your muscle" line.
- **Daily food:** a 12-year-old eats about 2,000 kcal/day, so 10,000 kcal is about five days of food:
  "eat a week of food" in the end line is a round-up, and the description should say "about a week".
